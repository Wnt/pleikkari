# PLE-715: what puts the Go's VrApi into its every-frame `Early` regime

## Answer

`Early=72`/`Prd=46ms` at 72 Hz (`Early=60`/`Prd=55ms` at 60 Hz) is VrApi's frame scheduler
running **one refresh ahead**: every frame is ready a whole refresh before the slot it was given.
It adds one refresh to the video frame's path from latch to photon: 14 ms at 72 Hz, 17 ms at 60 Hz.

* **The scheduler (read from the Go's own `libvrapiimpl.so`, VrDriver 18.0.0.1261.517).**
  `vrapi_SubmitFrame2` gives each frame a vsync slot, `clamp(previous slot + 1, now, now + 2)`.
  The next frame's `vrapi_GetPredictedDisplayTime` is built on that slot. The submit sleeps
  until a fixed phase of the next refresh, so while the app submits once per refresh the slot's
  lead over "now" stays as it is. A submit that comes after that phase goes through at once, and
  so does the next one, even in the same refresh: two submits in one refresh add a refresh of lead.
  PhaseSync, which would fix this, is only enabled with out-of-process composition, and the Go
  (device type 0x40) does not have it. `Lat=0` in every capture, so the SDK's extra-latency
  mode is not involved.
* **What triggers it:** the first frames of a VrApi session. Frame 1 takes 25-34 ms (first draw)
  and frame 3 takes 10-16 ms, and then a fast frame goes into the same refresh as a late one.
  Five of seven 72 Hz previews with the default loop (one of them with the debug `hold`) and the
  live 72 Hz stream settled at lead 1 within 100 ms of entering VR. In the two that did not, an
  early submit (frames 2-4) waited 11-42 ms instead (`summaries/s1-*-startup.txt`).
* **Why it never ends:** the lead only falls through the clamp, and a stall followed by a quick
  frame refills it at once. One-shot stalls of 14, 15, 30, 45 and 60 ms each returned to lead 1
  within two frames (`summaries/s1-p72-stalls-transitions.txt`). PLE-698's 72 Hz preview ran
  `Early=0` because its session happened to start at lead 0.

## Can the cinema's loop stay out of it: yes, `stream_go_vr_late_start`

The setting is off by default and applies only to the plain cinema (`vr-frame-pacing.c`):
* The loop sleeps before the latch, until 7.5 ms before VrApi's next release, on a grid anchored
  on the predicted display times (release = predicted − (lead + 1.38) refreshes). Today the
  frame waits that time inside the submit, after the latch.
* After a late frame, the next frame waits for that point rather than going into the same refresh.
* When 36 frames in a row run at lead 1 or more, the loop skips one release, which costs one
  repeated frame. It does this at most every 2 s.

### The sleep sweep (session 1, `summaries/s1-p72-sweep.txt`)

The loop slept 0 to 13.5 ms before the latch, in 0.5 ms steps every 2 s, starting at lead 0:
* **Up to 8.5 ms of sleep:** VrApi still paced the loop at the real refresh, 13.92 ms (71.82 Hz, not
  13.889). Predicted display minus release stayed at 19.2 ms (1.38 refreshes). Latch→photon fell
  one for one with the sleep, 32.2 → 24.0 ms. `Stale=0` held up to 7.5 ms.
* **From 8.0 ms of sleep:** the submit came less than ~3 ms before the release, and frames went stale.
* **Never below ~2 ms:** `vrapi_SubmitFrame2` returns no sooner than that. A first late start with
  a 5 ms budget sat inside that minimum and drifted into Early (session 2's first build).
* **At 9 ms and above:** the loop could not hold the refresh and flipped to lead 1.

## Not proven

* The rooms (void, cinema, terrace). Their eye frame takes about 8 ms of GPU, which the 7.5 ms
  budget does not leave, so the setting keeps today's loop while a room is drawn (`GoCinema: Frame
  pacing: VrApi release (late start is for the plain cinema; a room is drawn)`). Neither the drain
  alone nor a room budget was measured.
* A live 60 Hz stream with the late start (only the 60 Hz preview ran).
* Longer live runs: 40 s per arm. The stale rate and the drain's behaviour over a long session,
  and under network loss, are open.
* Anything a person sees: nobody wore the Go. Predicted photon is VrApi's prediction, not a
  photodiode.
* The head-tracking path. The timewarp re-projects with its own late pose (`TW=` 1.6 ms), so head
  rotation does not carry the `Prd` difference the video does; `Prd` itself falls 46 → 26 ms.
