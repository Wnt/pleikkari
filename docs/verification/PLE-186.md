# PLE-186 — Touched-arm operating-rate A/B at n=4: resolving the queue→present noise (2026-09-27, 16:22–16:50 UTC)

Follow-up of [`PLE-172.md`](PLE-172.md), which promoted `stream_decoder_operating_rate_default` to on. At n=2,
PLE-172 found touched queue→present avg "noisy" (one treatment run 0.49 ms above the baseline band) and called
it same-round drift. This round repeats only the touched arm, with n=4 per arm, run interleaved.

**Headline: this is not drift. The q→p shift is real, and the setting is still a net win.** Touched q→present avg
rises by about +0.38 ms (base 7.33–7.73, mean 7.49; rate 7.68–8.11, mean 7.87). Decode mean drops by about 1.2 ms
(5.93–6.16 → 4.72–4.81). Their sum, codec input → present, is lower on every treatment run
(base 13.29–13.72, mean 13.53; rate 12.47–12.83, mean 12.65: about −0.9 ms, and the bands do not overlap).
The likely reading: faster decode hands frames to `queueBuffer` earlier, while the next latch/present vsync
stays where it was. The frame then waits longer in the queue. q→p measures from `queueBuffer`, so it grows even
though the frame reaches the screen sooner. No change to the default.

## Verdict

The PLE-172 caveat is resolved. The touched q→p avg rise (about +0.4 ms) reproduces and is caused by the setting.
It is not drift. It is more than paid for by the decode drop, because q→p starts at the codec output, which
now comes earlier. The end-to-end codec input → present time drops by about 0.9 ms on every run. Keep
`stream_decoder_operating_rate_default=true`. When comparing arms that change decode time, compare
decode + q→p, not q→p alone.
