# PLE-753: do the Go's rooms fit VrApi's lead 0 with the hold and drain?

## Answer

**At 4x MSAA they do not.** Held at lead 0 (`Prd` 32 ms, `Early` 0), the void, cinema and
terrace drop 6 to 12 frames a second (8 to 17 %), even at the 510 MHz the runtime picks by
itself. Only lead 1, the every-frame `Early` regime, is clean (`Prd` 46 ms).

That is why the rooms looked clean in some runs and not in others:
* Which lead a session settles at is chance (PLE-715). In session 1 the void landed at lead 1
  by itself and showed 0 stale frames; the cinema and terrace landed at lead 0 and showed
  190-310 in 33 s.
* PLE-700's and PLE-701's baselines saw the same split.

**What a frame at lead 0 has to fit:** from its start (the moment VrApi releases the last
submit) to its submit, the CPU work, plus VrApi's `App=` GPU time, must stay under about
10.5 ms. From 11 ms up the stale share climbs: 3 % at 11-11.5 ms, 6.5 % at 12-12.5, 20 % at
13-13.5 (`summaries/budget-s1-s2.txt`, 380 lead-0 seconds from 15 arms).

A room adds about 2.5 ms of CPU before its submit (the preview's start to submit is 5.0 ms,
the plain screen's 2.7):
* 1.3 ms in the loop before `draw()`;
* 2.8 ms of GL commands inside `draw()`;
* 1.0 ms in the final `glFlush`.

The GPU starts on the eye buffers only after that. PLE-700's live cinema log read 3.7 ms start
to submit (no preview picture drawn on the loop thread).

**What fits:**

| room, held at lead 0 (preview) | GPU clock | `App=` | start to submit + `App=` | stale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cinema 4x | 510 MHz (runtime's choice) | 7.40 ms | 12.5 ms | 224 in 32 s (7.0/s) |
| cinema 4x, `stream_go_vr_room_high_gpu` | 510 MHz (pinned) | 7.40 ms | 12.5 ms | 207 (6.5/s) |
| cinema 4x, debug `flusheyes` | 401 MHz | 8.74 ms | (GPU starts before the submit) | 137 (4.3/s) |
| cinema 2x | 315 MHz (runtime's choice) | 6.92 ms | 11.9 ms | 56 (1.75/s) |
| void 2x | 315 MHz | 6.96 ms | 12.2 ms | 85 (2.7/s) |
| terrace 2x | 315 MHz | 6.96 ms | 12.3 ms | 106 (3.3/s) |
| cinema, MSAA off | 315 MHz | 4.42 ms | 9.3 ms | 7 (0.2/s) |

* A higher GPU level adds nothing at 4x: at lead 0 the runtime already runs a 4x room at
  510 MHz on its own.
* At 2x the runtime drops back to 315 MHz and the frame no longer fits. At 510 MHz 2x should
  read about 4.3 ms (6.92 × 315 / 510) and fit, but that was not measured (below).
* PLE-700's space lounge at 2x (`App=` 5.4 ms) and PLE-701's hall at 1x (3.6 ms) already fit.
  Their failed acceptance was the chance regime (lead 1, `Prd` 46), not their GPU cost; with
  the hold and drain they would stay at lead 0.

## Recommendation (head loop first)

* **Keep the rooms at lead 0 on purpose, not by chance.** Lead 1 costs the room's picture
  14 ms of `Prd` (46 against 32) and one refresh of video latency. Which lead a session gets
  is decided in its first frames (PLE-715), so today the same room is clean one run and drops
  frames the next. `stream_go_vr_hold_drain` holds lead 0.
* **Then make the room's frame fit, start to submit + `App=` ≲ 10.5 ms,** with one of:
  * **2x MSAA at GPU level 4:** `stream_go_vr_room_msaa=2` plus `stream_go_vr_room_high_gpu`.
    About 4.3 ms of `App=` by the clock ratio, keeping some edge smoothing. The candidate
    for PLE-747's A/B; not yet measured.
  * **MSAA off:** 4.4 ms at 315 MHz and 0.2 % stale in the preview, the cheapest in battery
    and heat, with the most aliasing.
* **Why not a higher GPU level alone:** it does nothing at 4x, because the runtime already
  runs a 4x room at 510 MHz at lead 0.
* **Why not lead 1 for rooms:** it is clean, but it is the `Prd` regression PLE-700 and PLE-701
  must not have.
* **PLE-700 and PLE-701:** their rooms already fit lead 0 (space 2x 5.4 ms, hall 1x 3.6 ms).
  Re-run their acceptance with `stream_go_vr_hold_drain` on.
* **"Zero stale" is too strict at lead 0:** even the plain screen sees the loop's CPU spikes
  (start to submit max 10-20 ms in these traces; PLE-715's late start showed 0.4 %). An
  acceptance of `Prd` 32-33 with at most ~0.5 % stale is one that lead 0 can meet.
* **The rooms' CPU before the GPU starts is the next lever.**
  * A room's frame spends 2.8 ms in `draw()`'s GL commands and 1.0 ms in its `glFlush`, and the
    GPU starts only after that.
  * Flushing after the first eye (`flusheyes`) cut stale frames from 224 to 137 while the
    runtime even picked a lower clock.
  * Each eye also attaches and detaches the room's depth renderbuffer on the swap-chain
    framebuffer every frame (`vr-environment.cpp` `draw_eye`), which makes the driver
    revalidate a multisampled framebuffer twice a frame.

## What changed

* **`stream_go_vr_hold_drain`** (off by default): PLE-715's hold and drain without the late
  latch, with the plain screen and with a room (`vr-frame-pacing.c`, `PLEIKKARI_VR_PACING_HOLD`).
  * A frame still starts as soon as the last submit returns, so a room keeps the whole refresh.
  * After a late frame the next one starts at VrApi's next release, at least 3 ms away.
  * 36 frames in a row at a lead of 1 or more skip one release, at most every 2 s.
  * With the late start on as well, the plain screen takes the late start and a room the hold
    and drain.
* **`stream_go_vr_room_msaa`** (4x by default, 2x or off): the rooms' sample count from the next
  cinema start. The preview's `environment_msaa` extra (PLE-653) still overrides it.
* **Settings fix:** PLE-715's `stream_go_vr_late_start` and PLE-755's `stream_go_vr_warm_up`
  switches were missing from Settings' `DataStore`, so toggling them never stored a change.
  They are mapped now, with a Robolectric test over every Go switch
  (`GoVrSettingsDataStoreTest`).
* **Debug trace:** `debug.pleikkari.vr_pacing` lines now split the frame start to submit (`d`
  start to `draw()`, `g` `draw()` to its `glFlush`, `f` the `glFlush`). The item `flusheyes`
  flushes after the first eye as well; `holddrain` selects the new mode.
* **Scripts:**
  * `docs/verification/PLE-715/go-pacing.sh` takes string prefs (`key=s:value`), a per-arm
    `@msaa=N` for the preview, `ENVIRONMENT=` (the room from the prefs) and `FULL_POSE=1`
    (screencap plus `screen_check.py` per arm).
  * `rooms.py` summarises arms.
  * `budget.py` bins lead-0 seconds by start to submit + `App=`.

## Measurements

* **Device:** Oculus Go `1KWPH802EW8203`,
  `oculus/vr_pacific/pacific:7.1.1/NGI77B/20008400174500000`, 1024x1024 eyes, 72 Hz. VrApi
  lines read `PSM=0`, 39 °C throughout.
* **Setup:** debug preview (synthetic 60 fps picture drawn on the cinema thread), 40 s arms,
  first 5 s skipped, `debug.pleikkari.vr_pacing=trace`, `vr_full_pose=1`.
  * Every arm's screencap passed `screen_check.py` (`summaries/screen-check-s1-s2.txt`).
  * Backup, `install -r` and restore wrapped each session under the Go lease. The original APK
    and prefs were back after each.
* **Session 1** (13:07-13:15 UTC, APK `3640ebc7`), `summaries/s1-rooms.txt`, each room with
  VrApi's loop and with the hold and drain:

  | room | loop | leads 0/1 | `Prd` | `App=` | stale in 33 s | `Early` |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | cinema | VrApi | 1943/288 | 32 | 7.35 ms | 190 | 182 |
  | cinema | hold and drain | 2300/0 | 32 | 7.43 ms | 263 | 0 |
  | void | VrApi | 3/2373 | **46** | 9.61 ms (401 MHz) | **0** | 209 of 2376 frames |
  | void | hold and drain | 2330/39, 1 drain | 32 | 7.33 ms | 320 | 32 |
  | terrace | VrApi | 2304/0 | 32 | 7.48 ms | 310 | 0 |
  | terrace | hold and drain | 2376/0 | 32 | 7.47 ms | 397 | 0 |

  VrApi's `Early` is not a lead gauge for a room: it counts a frame only when its GPU work too
  is done a refresh before its slot. The void at lead 1 (`App=` 9.6 ms) read `Early` 6 a second,
  not 72. Read `Prd` or the pacing line's leads.
* **Session 2** (13:19-13:26 UTC, APK `6c46b8f5`, all held at lead 0), `summaries/s2-levers.txt`:
  the table under "What fits".
* **Session 3** (13:33 UTC) produced nothing. The Go was on battery at 7 %, and the system
  `low_battery` alert (VrAlertService, since 13:32:29) refused every `am start`.
* **Raw logcats and screencaps** (host, untracked): `build/ple753/go-s{1,2,3}-*/<arm>/` in the
  worktree.

## Not proven

* **A live PS5 stream:** every figure here is the preview, whose synthetic picture is drawn with
  `lockHardwareCanvas` on the cinema thread (CPU and GPU the live stream does not spend). PLE-701
  saw live decoding cost more than the preview did.
* **2x MSAA with GPU level 4**, the likely fix, was queued for session 3 and not run.
* **Long sessions and heat:** a pinned 510 MHz costs battery and heat; not measured.
* **What a wearer sees:** 2x or no MSAA shows more aliasing on the rooms' edges, and nobody wore
  the Go.
